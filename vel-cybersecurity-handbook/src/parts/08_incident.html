Part 8 — Security Incident Response

This part trains employees to respond calmly and consistently. The department supports the client's incident response; it does not make legal decisions and does not take actions outside its contract.

8.3 Escalation procedure

  1. Detect and record: note exact time (with time zone), what you saw, which asset, who told you.
  2. Tell your Security Lead at once (phone, then message). Do not wait to be sure.
  3. Assign provisional severity using 8.2; the Security Lead confirms.
  4. Notify the client's authorised contact through the agreed channel; the PM keeps a timeline.
  5. Decide containment with the client system owner. VEL recommends; the owner approves changes on their systems.
  6. Escalate to legal/compliance: the Security Lead alerts the client's legal or compliance contact when personal data, regulated data or third parties may be affected.
  7. Record every action in the incident log; close with a lessons-learned review.

8.4 Evidence preservation

Requirement
☐Do not power off or rebuild an affected system until the owner and Security Lead decide; memory and running state may be needed.
☐Work on copies. Preserve originals; copy logs, images and files to the restricted store.
☐Hash and record. Compute SHA-256 (sha256sum file) when collected; note collector, date/time, time zone and source.
☐Chain of custody: every transfer logged (from, to, when, why). Fields in Appendix C.
☐Time synchronisation: note clock offsets between systems so timelines are reliable.
☐Minimise personal data: collect only what the investigation needs; restrict access.
☐No unauthorised "hack back" or contact with the suspected attacker.

8.5 Client notification responsibilities

VEL doesVEL does not
  • Tell the client's authorised contact promptly and factually
  • Provide timeline, facts known, unknowns, and recommended containment
  • Provide evidence in the agreed format
  • Remind the client to involve legal/compliance and, where applicable, their regulator-facing contact
  • Keep a written record of what was communicated and when
  • Decide whether a breach must be notified to a regulator, customers or the public
  • Contact regulators, media or affected individuals on the client's behalf unless contractually instructed in writing
  • Give legal conclusions or speculate on cause or blame
  • Share incident details with anyone outside the authorised list
For awareness only: notification clocks exist

Some laws and directions set short deadlines, for example CERT-In directions in India (six hours for specified cyber incident types), the DPDP Act, 2023, and GDPR's 72-hour rule for personal data breaches. VEL never decides if or when these apply. The Security Lead alerts the client and their counsel immediately so they can decide in time.

8.6 Incidents during an engagement

  • If you find signs of a real compromise, treat it as a stop-work condition (Part 3.8): stop, preserve what you saw, call the Security Lead.
  • If your testing causes an outage, stop, tell the client emergency contact at once, assist restoration and document honestly. Early honesty is protected.
  • If you encounter unexpected personal data, stop, do not copy it, and report; the data handling agreement governs next steps.

8.7 Tabletop exercise 01 (Training Week 8)

Scenario (fictional): a small accounting firm reports that three staff cannot open files and a note demands payment. One staff member says they clicked a link yesterday.

#Questions the group must answer
1What do we know, and what do we not know? What is the provisional severity and why?
2Whom do we tell first, second and third? Who approves containment?
3What evidence must be preserved before any action, and who may touch the machines?
4Which backups exist, and how would we verify them before restoring?
5What do we say to the client contact, and what must we not say (legal decisions, blame, payment advice)?
6What lessons and control changes would we recommend afterwards?

Scoring: participants are assessed on following escalation, evidence discipline, clear communication and respecting limits (Part 5.5).
