Part 3 — Authorised Testing Policy (POL-SEC-001)

Policy IDPOL-SEC-001 · Version 1.0 · effective on management approval
Applies toAll VEL employees, contractors and trainees who perform or support security assessment, scanning, testing, hardening or monitoring
Policy ownerSecurity Lead · approved by Founder
Breach of policyMay result in disciplinary action, termination and legal liability. Unauthorised access to computer systems can be a criminal offence.
The one-sentence policy

We test, scan or access only systems that the owner has authorised in writing, only the targets, methods and times named in that authorisation, and only by people whose authority level permits the activity.

3.1 Purpose and principles

  1. Legality: security testing without permission can break criminal and civil law, even with good intentions.
  2. Safety: testing can slow, crash or corrupt systems and expose personal data. The client's business comes first.
  3. Proportionality: use the least intrusive method that answers the question.
  4. Accountability: every action can be traced to a person, an approval and a record.

No scan, probe, login or test of a client system may start until every item is complete and filed. The QA Reviewer checks the pack before the Security Lead releases the "Authorised to start" marker.

3.4 Testing is not allowed if…

  • the target is not on the approved list, even if it "looks like" the client's;
  • the authorisation is expired, unsigned, or signed by someone without authority over the asset;
  • the target is run by a third party who has not agreed (cloud provider rules, SaaS vendors, shared hosting neighbours);
  • a stop-work condition is active; or
  • you are unsure. Uncertainty is a stop, not a green light.

3.6 Mandatory safe-testing practice

  1. Re-read the RoE at the start of each session; confirm the date, window and target list.
  2. Verify the target against approved-targets.csv; check IP/domain ownership before running a tool. DNS can change.
  3. Choose the gentlest tool setting. Set rate limits and thread counts; exclude dangerous checks (e.g. destructive or denial-of-service plugins).
  4. Use a recorded, approved workstation (Stage 07): encrypted disk, MFA, logging, tool versions recorded.
  5. Log every run: who, what, command/settings, target, start/end time, result file name.
  6. Verify manually only where safe and permitted; stop at the minimum proof needed. Do not read, copy or modify real customer data beyond what the RoE allows.
  7. Handle credentials correctly: use test accounts; store in the approved password manager; never reuse; never place in tickets, chats or repositories.
  8. Report promptly: a Critical finding that is actively exploitable or exposes personal data is reported to the Security Lead immediately, then to the client contact the same day through the agreed channel.
  9. Clean up: remove test accounts, uploaded files and scan artefacts as agreed; confirm in writing.

3.7 Prohibited actions (regardless of who asks)

Never
✗Test a system for "practice" or "to impress" without written authority, including your former employer, friends' sites, public bug-bounty targets outside their published programme rules, or government/critical infrastructure.
✗Run unreviewed scripts, exploits or tools downloaded from the internet on a client system or on the company network.
✗Guess, brute-force or reuse passwords on accounts you were not given for the test.
✗Deploy malware, backdoors or persistence mechanisms on any client system.
✗Access, download or share real customer personal data beyond the minimum evidence agreed.
✗Exceed the time window, rate limits or methods in the RoE; or continue after a stop-work call.
✗Disclose vulnerabilities to anyone but the authorised client contacts and VEL's named reviewers.
✗Commit evidence, passwords, tokens, client data or exploitable detail to an ordinary Git repository, personal drive, messaging app or AI chat tool.

3.8 Stop-work conditions

Stop all active testing at once (do not "just finish this one request"), preserve notes, and call the Security Lead. The Security Lead informs the client's emergency contact.

  • Target slows, errors, or becomes unavailable during testing.
  • You discover a target not on the list, or the owner of a target is not the client.
  • You encounter personal, financial or health data not covered by the data handling agreement.
  • You find signs of a real attack or prior compromise (unknown accounts, unexpected processes, webshells).
  • The client contact or VEL lead calls stop, or the time window ends.
  • A tool behaves unexpectedly (sends traffic you did not intend).
  • A legal, safety or contractual doubt arises.

3.9 Evidence and data handling

  • Collect the minimum evidence that proves the issue: sanitised screenshots, truncated logs, configuration excerpts. Mask secrets, tokens and personal data.
  • Store evidence only in the approved, access-controlled, encrypted store. Record a SHA-256 hash and collection time for important items.
  • Retain and delete according to the signed data handling agreement and policies/retention.md; record deletion.
  • Reports for executives must not contain unnecessary exploit detail. Technical detail goes in a restricted annex with need-to-know access.

3.10 Training and lab rule

All practice for learning, tool testing or demonstrations uses owned or intentionally vulnerable environments, built on an isolated network (Part 9), with a signed Lab Authorisation Record (Appendix C). Intentionally vulnerable apps (for example OWASP Juice Shop) are run locally and never exposed to the internet or company LAN. Training skills are never tried on "real" targets.

3.11 Disclosure, incidents and breach of policy

  • Vulnerabilities found are disclosed only to the client's authorised contact via the agreed secure channel.
  • Accidental out-of-scope contact (for example a mistyped IP) is reported to the Security Lead immediately, with exact time and action. Honest early reporting is protected; concealment is a serious breach.
  • Policy violations are investigated by the Security Lead and Founder. Outcomes range from retraining to dismissal and, where required, legal reporting.
