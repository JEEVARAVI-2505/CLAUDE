0.1 What this handbook is

This handbook sets up a professional cybersecurity services department inside VEL Software Solutions. It tells every employee what the department does, who may do what, how beginners are trained, and how a client engagement runs from first call to handover. It is written for people who start with basic IT knowledge and must become safe, effective defenders.

It is delivered one module at a time, because quality and legal safety matter more than speed. This first module contains the six items needed before any security work begins.

0.3 How to use it

  1. Read Part 3 (Authorised Testing Policy) and sign the acknowledgement before touching any tool.
  2. Follow Part 4 for your first week, then the 90-day roadmap in Part 5.
  3. Check Part 2 to learn what you may do alone, what needs approval, and what you must never do.
  4. When a client project starts, work through the stages in Part 7 in order and do not skip a gate.
  5. If you are unsure, stop and ask your Security Lead. Stopping is never a mistake; guessing about authorisation is.

0.4 The twelve golden rules

  1. No written authorisation, no testing. Verbal permission, a friendly email, or "it is our own client's website" is not authorisation.
  2. Stay inside the scope. Only the exact targets, methods and time window written in the Rules of Engagement.
  3. Practise only in owned or intentionally vulnerable labs. Learning never happens on real third-party systems.
  4. Beginners do not perform high-risk testing alone and never approve their own critical findings.
  5. Do no harm. Prefer the least intrusive method that answers the question. Back up before changing anything.
  6. Stop first, explain later if something unexpected happens: an outage, real customer data, signs of a real attacker.
  7. Verify before you report. A scanner alert is a lead, not a finding.
  8. Protect evidence and client data. Encrypt it, minimise it, never put it in an ordinary Git repository.
  9. Tell the truth in reports. No exaggeration, no hidden limitations, no invented certainty.
  10. Never claim what we do not hold. No certification, audit authority, "guaranteed secure", 24/7 SOC or structural-style safety claims we cannot back up.
  11. Never make legal decisions. Breach notification and legal interpretation belong to the client and qualified legal or compliance specialists.
  12. Write it down. If it is not in a ticket, a log or a document, it did not happen.
Important legal notice

This handbook is an internal operating procedure, not legal advice. Laws on computer misuse, data protection and incident reporting differ by country and change over time. The department must confirm applicable requirements with qualified legal counsel (for India this includes the Information Technology Act, 2000, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and CERT-In directions) before offering services or signing client contracts. Claims policy

VEL does not claim certification, independent-audit authority, accreditation, or 24/7 monitoring unless the company and named personnel actually hold the credentials and have the staffing and systems to deliver. Every proposal, website line and report must be checked against this rule by the Security Lead.
