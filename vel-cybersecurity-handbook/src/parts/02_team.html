2.2 The authority ladder (what each person may do alone)

Authority depends on demonstrated competence, not job title. Each level is granted in writing by the Security Lead after the gates in Part 5.

LevelNameMay doMust not do
A0TraineeLearn and practise only in the owned isolated lab (Part 9) and intentionally vulnerable training apps. Write documentation. Shadow engagements as observer.Touch any client system, even read-only. Run any scan on a non-lab network.
A1Supervised juniorOn an authorised engagement, with a named senior present or reviewing live: Tier 0 and Tier 1 work (Part 3). Draft findings.Tier 2 or 3 testing. Close or rate Critical/High findings. Contact the client technically without the Consultant.
A2PractitionerTier 0 and 1 independently; Tier 2 inside an approved window with written approval from Security Lead. Own findings drafts and hardening tasks.Tier 3. Approve their own Critical/High findings. Change scope.
A3Senior / LeadRecommend and run Tier 3 only where management has enabled the service and the client has specifically authorised it. Approve findings, reports, scope.Operate outside contracted scope. Make legal notification decisions.
Hard rule

A beginner (A0 or A1) must never independently perform high-risk security testing and must never approve their own critical findings. Critical and High findings need a second person (Consultant or Security Lead) to confirm before the client sees them.

2.5 Separation of duties

  • Authors do not approve their own Critical or High findings or reports.
  • The person who performs a hardening change is not the sole verifier of that change.
  • Access to client evidence is granted per engagement, reviewed monthly, and removed at close-out.
  • Administrative access to VEL's evidence store is held by the Security Lead and one named deputy only.
