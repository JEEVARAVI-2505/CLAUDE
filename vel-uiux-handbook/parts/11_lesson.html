Size, weight, colour and space say: headline, support, action, extra.
1. What is it?The order in which the eye notices things on a screen, created by size, weight, colour, contrast, position and space.
2. Why does it matter?Users scan, they do not read. Hierarchy tells them what is most important and what to do next.
3. Where is it used?Every screen: websites, apps, dashboards, forms, documents, slide decks.
4. Business problem solvedUsers miss the main action or message, so tasks take longer or are abandoned. (We measure this in testing; we do not promise a percentage.)
5. Tools neededFigma (text styles, Auto Layout), a contrast checker.
6. Install and configureSection 4 set-up. Add Inter font; create text styles H1, H2, Body, Caption.
7. Steps
  1. Write the one thing the user must notice first.
  2. Make it the largest or heaviest element.
  3. Choose second and third levels; give each a distinct size/weight.
  4. Group related items with space (use the 8px scale).
  5. Give the primary action the only magenta fill.
  6. Squint test: blur the screen; the order must still read.
8. Expected outputA frame with exactly 3 text levels, 1 primary action, consistent spacing, saved as styles.
9. VerifySquint test with a colleague (can they say the main message in 5 seconds?); check contrast ratios; compare against checklist.
10. Common mistakesToo many bold items; five font sizes; colour used for decoration; centred long paragraphs; two competing primary buttons.
11. Professional standardsMax 3-4 text sizes per screen; body ≥ 16px on mobile; line length 45-75 characters; one primary action per view.
12. DocumentationA note in design-decisions.md: "Headline is level 1 because ...".
13. Practical assignmentRedesign the "poor" card above for a real local business of your choice. Deliver before/after frames.
14. EvaluationRubric (Section 9): hierarchy 20, typography/colour 15, documentation 10 etc.
15. ApproverMentor (Week 1); Senior Product Designer for Project 1.
Module wrap-up (appears at the end of every module) Summary: hierarchy = size + weight + colour + space, ordered by importance. Assignment: item 13. Checklist: 3 levels · 1 primary action · styles saved · contrast checked · decision noted. Assessment: quiz questions 1-3 in Appendix C. Deliverable: before/after Figma frames + note. Approval criteria: squint test passed, no rubric item below 60 %.
